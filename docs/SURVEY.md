# Certify What Tolerant Kernels Guess

akernel's best route past Parasolid and xNURBS is not breadth. It is to certify the cases that the incumbents either guess at or quietly tolerate: tangent and degenerate surface/surface intersections, coincidence, the effects of tolerance drift, and curvature-continuous fills. On each of these the published record shows a real gap. The strongest recent SSI work, Yang, Jia and Yan's topology-guaranteed B-spline intersection (SIGGRAPH Asia 2023), shows OCCT and ACIS returning wrong topology on near-tangent inputs ([SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/topology-guaranteed-b-spline-surface-surface-intersection-by-yang-jia-and-yan/)). Even that paper does not visibly certify every step. Certified deflation (Hauenstein–Mourrain–Szanto 2015; Mantzaflaris–Mourrain–Szanto 2023) is the missing tool that would let akernel's existing Krawczyk machinery prove isolated tangencies instead of refusing them ([arXiv 1501.05083](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1501.05083)). On surfacing, xNURBS publicly documents G2 only ([xNURBS](https://www.xnurbs.com/)), and its vendor quotes about 0.001 mm and 0.1° accuracy, which implies approximate rather than exact continuity ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/xnurbs-releases-a-ground-breaking-nurbs-software/49049)). The surfacing notes found no published work that certifies G2/G3 seam continuity with rigorous bounds. A Bernstein-form seam certificate for akernel's degree-7 fill is therefore cheap, new, and immediately distinctive. The hard truth is scale. akernel has been tested only on 6-face single-patch solids, and the scaling techniques it needs (arrangement-then-label Booleans, indirect predicates, conservative broad phases, STEP import, ABC-scale benchmarks) are well documented but still to be built. The roadmap below therefore puts the cheap, distinctive certification work first and the long scaling work after it. It ends with a black-box protocol that can compare akernel's fills with xNURBS rigorously, because exported NURBS are exact data.

A note on markers. Citations keep the research notes' verification status. Pre-2015 work is labelled **[FOUNDATIONAL]**. **[PARTLY VERIFIED]** names the detail not confirmed. **UNVERIFIED** and **DOI unverified** are carried over verbatim. Inferences about akernel are this report's engineering judgment, not claims made by the cited sources. One observation comes from akernel's code itself: `include/akernel/num/interval.hpp` widens bounds with `std::nextafter` rather than switching rounding modes.

## Topology-guaranteed SSI exists, but nobody certifies tangency end to end

The closest published blueprint for what akernel lacks is **Yang, Jia and Yan, "Topology Guaranteed B-Spline Surface/Surface Intersection," ACM TOG 42(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2023, DOI 10.1145/3618349**. It claims correct topology for cross intersections, **isolated contact points, boundary intersections, contacts between branches, and high-order contact along a curve**. Those are exactly the configurations akernel currently refuses. It benchmarks efficiency and topological correctness against SISL, OCCT and ACIS ([SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/learning/topology-guaranteed-b-spline-surface-surface-intersection-by-yang-jia-and-yan/); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3618349)). A catalogue record and the authors' slides describe the method as implicitization, box tests and curve-topology determination ([slides](https://yangjieyin.github.io/homepage/ACMTOG2023/SIGA23-slides.pdf)). The internal certification is still unverified, though. Whether special points are certified with intervals or exact arithmetic, or found with floating-point thresholds, was not visible on any page the researchers could open. Reading the full paper is therefore the first task before adopting it.

Across the topology-guaranteed literature, the 2023 TOG paper, a 2026 algebraic-surface paper ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40304-026-00499-0)) and the 2012 homeomorphic-approximation paper ([arXiv 1203.0442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.0442)) share one four-step recipe. First, reduce the intersection to a plane curve, either in one patch's parameter domain or by projection. Second, isolate every special point: singular, turning and boundary points. Third, certify the regular arcs between them. Fourth, connect the arcs at the special points using local topology. **akernel already has step three** in its Krawczyk tubes. Steps one, two and four are the missing layers, and they are what turns "refuse at tangency" into "classify at tangency."

For the plane-curve stage there are two mature certified families. The exact or symbolic-numeric family includes Bisolve/GeoTop ([arXiv 1201.1548](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1201.1548)) and **PTOPO (JSC 115, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.jsc.2022.08.012)**. PTOPO computes an isotopic graph of a parametric curve, including singularities and their multiplicities, in Õ_B(N⁶) ([arXiv 2101.01925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01925v1)). The subdivision family runs from Plantinga–Vegter to Burr, Choi, Galehouse and Yap. Their method is described as the first complete purely numerical isotopic meshing of curves with isolated singularities, and it works by isolating singular points before subdividing ([arXiv 1102.5463](https://arxiv.org/html/1102.5463)). The subdivision family fits akernel's architecture better because it keeps box subdivision and adds a singular-point stage with root-bound termination. The exact family is the right fallback inside a box flagged as near-singular.

The algebraic alternative comes from Busé and coauthors' matrix representations. A patch becomes a matrix whose rank drops exactly on the surface. Substituting the other patch gives the intersection as a rank condition in one parameter domain, where a tangency shows up as a multiple factor or singular point of a plane curve ([Busé publications](http://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Laurent.Buse/publications.html)). The line-surface version costs a 6q²×8q² eigenproblem, which is **54×72 for bicubics**: feasible, but akernel would need its own dense SVD or eigensolver ([Xiao, Busé, Cirak 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nme.6136)). Matrix methods find near-multiple roots gracefully but do not certify them. They are a candidate generator, not a proof. The direct implicitize-and-substitute route also hits a degree wall: a bicubic patch has implicit degree 18, which is why practical systems stay in subdivision. The realistic design is therefore hybrid. Subdivide first, and run local algebraic analysis only in boxes flagged as near-singular.

Two cheaper aids sit on either side of the certificate. Park, Son, Kim and Elber's BVH with osculating toroidal leaves tracks second-order geometry, which cuts the subdivision blow-up that first-order boxes suffer near tangency ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010448520300592)). It is not certified, so it belongs in front of the Krawczyk test as a culling layer. Gao, Lin and Li's 2026 Mapper preprint groups intersection samples into a topology graph and splits the curve at the singular points it finds ([arXiv 2604.12631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12631)). It is a heuristic unless combined with certificates. The negative evidence matters just as much: the notes found **no public evidence that Parasolid, ACIS, CGM or OCCT use certified SSI with tangency handling**. Because those internals are proprietary, that absence is expected and does not prove anything.

## Deflation turns refused tangencies into certified ones

akernel's blocker is precise: an interval Krawczyk certificate cannot prove existence at a singular root, because the Jacobian is singular there. **Certified deflation removes that obstacle.** Hauenstein, Mourrain and Szanto (ISSAC 2015) build a square augmented system in which the original singular root becomes a simple root, while new variables encode the multiplicity structure. A standard Krawczyk or α-theory certificate then applies ([arXiv 1501.05083](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1501.05083); [project page](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~jhauenst/deflation/)). Mantzaflaris, Mourrain and Szanto (JSC 115, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.jsc.2022.08.006) give an iteration that converges **quadratically** to a triple of singular root, multiplicity structure and error bound. Plain Newton converges only linearly at multiple roots and cannot certify there ([arXiv 2002.05991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05991)). For an isolated point contact between two patches, or two edges tangent at a vertex, akernel can build the deflated system symbolically from its Bezier polynomials and run its *existing* Krawczyk operator on it.

There is a theoretical limit, and akernel should state it in its API. Floating-point certificates prove that a **nearby** system has a singular root, with an explicit perturbation bound ([Hauenstein–Szanto, arXiv 1212.4699](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4699)). Proving that the *exact* input is tangent needs exact algebra or a root-separation bound. akernel has an advantage here that most kernels lack. Its double control points are exact dyadic rationals, so the rational-coefficient route of "Certifying solutions to overdetermined and singular polynomial systems over ℚ" applies directly. That paper uses determinantal isosingular deflation that adds equations but no variables ([arXiv 1408.2721](https://arxiv.org/html/1408.2721v1)). Tangent SSI points satisfy an over-determined system (equal positions *and* parallel normals), so methods that certify over-determined systems directly are the natural formulation ([MDPI 2018](https://doi.org/10.3390/math6090166)).

Tangency along a curve is a different problem, because the root is not isolated and deflation does not apply. Here the tool is exact algebra. When two surfaces touch along a curve, the parameter-domain equation F(s,t) carries a squared factor, which is the "non-reduced" case of the space-curve topology literature ([arXiv 0803.1110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1110)). An exact squarefree decomposition (GCD of F with its partial derivatives) detects and extracts the contact curve. akernel's existing special case, the shared boundary line found by exact factorisation, is one instance of this general step. Generalising it is the concrete change.

Many subproblems collapse to one variable: a boundary curve of patch A against patch B, discriminants, and resultants along a line. For these, Bernstein-basis Descartes isolation is natural on Bezier data. **ANewDsc** (Kobel, Rouillier, Sagraloff, ISSAC 2016) shows how to detect clusters of near-multiple roots quickly and certify the output with MPFI intervals. It beats existing implementations on "notoriously hard" instances ([arXiv 1605.00410](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00410)). With cluster detection, akernel can report "a cluster of radius ε, possibly a double root" instead of failing. When the cluster is smaller than the separation bound for the polynomial's degree and bit size, it is provably one multiple root. The notes found **no SSI-specific separation bounds** at CAD degrees, which is an open measurement question.

Of the libraries, only a few are maintained and certified. **msolve** (ISSAC 2021; GPLv2) does exact zero-dimensional solving with real isolation ([arXiv 2104.03572](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03572); [GitHub](https://github.com/algebraic-solving/msolve)). **HomotopyContinuation.jl `certify`** does interval Krawczyk on square systems, evaluated as straight-line programs to limit overestimation, and is reported orders of magnitude faster than alphaCertified ([arXiv 2011.05000](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.05000v6)). Neither is header-only, and msolve's licence rules out embedding. Both are ideal **offline oracles**. Given the rational control points of two patches, msolve can enumerate every tangency point and the test suite can check that akernel's certified output is complete. The straight-line-program trick, by contrast, transfers directly into akernel's own interval evaluator.

## Arrangement-then-label beats per-pair Booleans, and tolerances are the enemy

The scalable exact Boolean has been worked out on meshes, and its structure carries over. Every method that scales, from Zhou et al. 2016 through Cherchi et al. 2020/2022 and EMBER to Lévy's exact CSG, **separates computing an exact arrangement of all boundary pieces from a global labelling pass**. Zhou, Grinspun, Zorin and Jacobson label each arrangement cell with a winding-number vector, one entry per operand. That generalises Booleans to n-ary and non-manifold inputs and tolerates slightly non-closed input ([project page](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements)). Cherchi et al. reach interactive robust Booleans, reportedly at least an order of magnitude faster than prior robust floating-point methods ([UniMoRe IRIS](https://iris.unimore.it/handle/11380/1299687)). EMBER uses fixed-width homogeneous integer coordinates with a proven bit budget and local subdivision with early-outs, and reports orders-of-magnitude speedups on Thingi10K ([RWTH](https://www.graphics.rwth-aachen.de/publication/03339)). For akernel the change is direct. Its per-pair Boolean on ~37-face results has to become "compute and imprint all certified face-face curves once, then label cells by winding vectors" before face counts reach the thousands.

The second transferable idea is **indirect predicates**. Attene (CAD 126, 2020) evaluates signs on the primitives that *define* a constructed point, with a floating-point filter and an expansion fallback, and never on the rounded point. He reports this faster than lazy-exact DAGs, with the caveat that it supports only **one level** of construction ([arXiv 2105.09772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09772v2)). That suggests a three-way vertex taxonomy for akernel. Type A vertices are exact dyadic or rational (already proven in akernel; the analogue of EMBER's bounded-bit integers). Type B vertices are freeform and indirect: a surface triple, or a surface pair plus a curve parameter, carried with an isolating interval box, with predicates run through an interval filter and then exact evaluation on the defining polynomials. Type C vertices are algebraic, for analytic surface pairs, handled exactly as in CGAL's arrangements on quadrics and cyclides ([Berberich et al. 2010](https://www.geometrie.tugraz.at/kerber/kerber_papers/paper2-concretizations.pdf)) and ESOLID ([UNC](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ESOLID)). The one-level limit also argues against chained constructions such as an edge from a vertex from an edge. CGAL's lazy scheme ([Pion–Fabri](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs.CG/0608063)) is the fallback when depth cannot be bounded. ESOLID's history adds a warning. Exact coordinates everywhere were too expensive, so **exactness should be confined to decisions (topology), not coordinates**.

On curved B-reps specifically, the field is thin. The notes found **no 2015–2026 paper claiming exact or fully certified Booleans for general trimmed NURBS solids at industrial scale**. The most visible recent system is Yang, Jia, Wang, Yang, Xin and Yan (ACM TOG 44(4), SIGGRAPH 2025, DOI 10.1145/3730908). It maps each B-rep bijectively to a mesh with controlled error and uses conservative mesh intersection to find every intersection curve. The authors claim watertight, correct results and better efficiency than OCCT, ACIS and Rhino, but give no failure rates ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3730908)). For akernel, the useful piece is the conservative-enclosure broad phase. Bezier hulls or bounded-error tessellations can **guarantee no missed intersection loop**, after which each loop is refined by certified tracing. The interval solid-modelling papers of Hu, Patrikalakis and Ye (1996–97) are akernel's direct ancestors. Their weakness, interval growth that leaves topology ambiguous, is exactly what akernel's exact coincidence predicates address ([record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:1372153)). The notes judge that on tangential and overlapping freeform contact, **akernel's exact coincidence predicates are already ahead of the published freeform work**. That is a publishable niche.

The case against stored tolerances is now documented in primary sources. OCCT's official Boolean specification declares interference when the distance between two entities is within the **sum** of their tolerances. Merged vertices become a sphere enclosing both tolerance spheres, and vertex-on-edge sets Tol(V) = max(Tol(V), D + Tol(E)). In other words, **tolerances only grow** ([OCCT spec](https://occt3d.com/dev/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)). Sum-of-tolerance interference is not transitive, so long chains of operations accumulate tolerance until unrelated entities "interfere." Parasolid has attached local tolerances to faces, edges and vertices since Jackson's 1995 paper, where a tolerant edge becomes a collection of curves ([Jackson 1995 PDF](https://ftp1.cs.wisc.edu/pub/users/prem/jackson-SM-95.pdf)). Those details come from search snippets because the PDF text could not be extracted. A Parasolid-based product's documentation states that tolerant bodies exported to IGES, STEP or STL still have gaps ([KeyCreator help](https://help.kubotekkosmos.com/KeyCreator/2026SP0/A10196)). Cubit warns that raising ACIS's tolerance runs ACIS "in a non-tested mode" ([Cubit docs](https://cubit.sandia.gov/files/cubit/17.10/help_manual/WebHelp/geometry/cleanup_and_defeaturing/geometry_accuracy.htm)). OCCT's gluing mode, where the user asserts coincidence and the kernel does not check it, is the inexact twin of akernel's exact coincidence predicates. **akernel can prove what OCCT must be told.** The price of no tolerances is that akernel cannot accept gappy imported data, which motivates the tolerant-import boundary in the STEP section below. Nehring-Wirxel, Trettner and Kobbelt also show the payoff of never re-rounding: no drift over hundreds of iterated CSG operations ([arXiv 2103.02486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02486v1)). akernel should reproduce that experiment on curved solids.

## Watertightness should move from samples into geometry

akernel's tessellator already enforces the right contract: topology is fixed and edge samples are shared. The literature's next step is to make the shared boundary part of the **surface representation**, not just the mesh. Li, Zhang, Guo, Xiao and Guo (arXiv 2402.10216) reparameterise each trimmed Bezier surface into a group of Bezier patches on [0,1]² and **replace each patch boundary with the intersection curve**, which eliminates trimming ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10216)). Urick et al. (CAD 115, 2019) do the same at framework level in three stages (parametric analysis, reparameterisation, model-space update). Their output is untrimmed patches of explicit continuity that reuse data from a conventional Boolean ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010448519302106)). Because akernel's faces are already Bezier, this fits it unusually well.

There is one irreducible choice. The true SSI curve generally has far higher degree than any practical Bezier, so akernel must pick between two designs. One keeps an exact implicit edge (two surfaces plus a certified tube) with shared samples, so the gap is zero but the geometry is algebraic. The other uses a **certified approximate Bezier edge** with reparameterised neighbouring faces, so the gap is bounded by a certificate rather than being zero. The second exports cleanly to STEP; the first is the ground truth. The defensible design keeps both: the implicit edge as the kernel's truth, and the reparameterised Bezier faces as a derived, exportable product carrying a certified gap bound. Hass, Farouki, Han, Song and Sederberg (2007) give the formal backing for coupling topology resolution and domain decomposition so that both faces see one consistent boundary ([PDF](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hass/Research/Papers/GeomDesign/Consistency.pdf)). Massarwi, van Sosin and Elber's untrimming (C&G 70, 2018) lifts curved quadrilateral sub-domains by surface-surface composition ([Technion PDF](https://gershon.cs.technion.ac.il/papers/untrimmed_srfs2018.pdf)). In akernel's polynomial setting **composition is exact**, so untrimming adds degree but no approximation error.

On the mesh side, two 2026 results set targets. Li, Luo, Feng, Zhang, Gai and Lei (JCDE 13(1), 2026, DOI 10.1093/jcde/qwaf134) use convex hulls of subpatches to define critical regions. They prove that sampled polylines and triangles in non-overlapping critical regions do not intersect under refinement, and were the **only method among NetGen, Gmsh and OCCT free of self-intersections on all benchmark cases** ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/13/1/239/8383411)). Bezier control polygons give akernel these hulls for free, which upgrades "watertight" to "watertight and embedded." Zhou, Zint, Izadyar, Tao, Panozzo and Schneider (arXiv 2604.02141, 2026) hold B-rep topology invariant during meshing and evaluate on thousands of ABC and Fusion 360 models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02141)). Their protocol is a ready-made scaling benchmark for akernel's tessellator. Spainhour and Weiss's generalized winding numbers for trimmed NURBS ([arXiv 2504.11435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11435)) add a cheap audit. On akernel's own watertight outputs the winding number must be **exactly integer away from the boundary**, and on imported gappy B-reps it still gives a robust containment test.

## xNURBS sells G2 by measured deviation; certified G3 is open ground

Publicly, xNURBS is a closed-source Rhino and SolidWorks plugin built on its own kernel. The vendor describes it as using an "energy-minimization method to generate the smoothest NURBS surface among all possible solutions." **Its official site names G2 only**, says nothing about G3, N-sided-specific methods, patents or publications, and markets millisecond solves with "unlimited capacity" ([xNURBS](https://www.xnurbs.com/)). Search snippets say an API has existed since V5.0 and that the kernel ships as `xnkernel.dll` ([Rhino blog 2021](https://blog.rhino3d.com/2021/09/xnurbs-rhino-plugin-api-package-c.html?m=0)). A claim that the kernel is "only available to ISV" is UNVERIFIED. The V5.2 release (January 2022) advertised "further reducing the **G1 deviation**," with no mention of G2 or G3 ([Rhino blog](https://blog.rhino3d.com/2022/01/xnurbs-rhino-plugin-v52-released.html)). In the 2017 launch thread the vendor representative claimed variational surfacing "on the same basis as CATIA," arbitrary curve and point constraints, N-sided patches as a trivial application, and **accuracy of about 0.001 mm and 0.1°**. One user measured "0.1163 max off G1" on an N-sided test, which beat Fusion 360, and another immediately asked about G2 deviation ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/xnurbs-releases-a-ground-breaking-nurbs-software/49049)). The notes found **no paper, patent or white paper** describing its algorithm. The most plausible reading, which is an inference, is a global quadratic energy minimisation with continuity imposed by penalty or least squares, whose results the market judges by measured deviation rather than by construction. The academic ancestor of "energy-based NURBS" is the Stony Brook dynamic-NURBS line ([Hui & Qin 2002](https://www3.cs.stonybrook.edu/~qin/research/hui-ijsm2002.pdf)) [FOUNDATIONAL].

The class-A incumbents set a higher bar than xNURBS advertises. Alias tutorials say G3 across patches "is often used" for class-A highlight flow, with G3 removing the curvature-comb "peak at the surface boundary." The same material warns that continuity checks alone are "not enough to judge the surface quality" ([Autodesk Alias](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2022/ENU/Alias-Tutorials/files/GUID-64611955-D2CC-44F2-98F0-D4F1FE931D8B.htm)). Karčiauskas and Peters operationalise class A as **non-oscillating highlight lines**. They show bicubics can reach it at irregular points if a normal mismatch of about **0.1°** is tolerated, the industry tolerance ([CGF 2015](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/research/SurfLab/papers/15bi3C0.pdf)). Together these give akernel two acceptance criteria to encode: a numeric G1 tolerance, and highlight-line oscillation as the real test.

For N-sided fills, two research lines dominate. The Várady–Salvi–Vaitkus ribbon and transfinite line is surveyed in CAGD 110 (2024) ([10.1016/j.cagd.2024.102286](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cagd.2024.102286)). It reaches G2 through parabolic ribbons ([Salvi & Várady 2014](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v33i7pp151-160)), has added interior control (2024, 2025) and curved multi-connected domains (2020, 2021), and has added cross-derivative optimisation for fairness ([Gunpinar et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gmod.2025.101275)). The Karčiauskas–Peters polynomial line fills holes in C2 bicubic complexes. Its 2015 functional-based biquintic G2 surfaces fix free coefficients by minimising fairness, which is **the closest published analogue to akernel's energy-fixes-free-jets approach** ([10.1016/j.cagd.2014.11.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cagd.2014.11.003)). Their 2020 result proves that refinable G2 multi-sided caps cannot be bi-5 and exhibits a bi-6 construction ([10.1016/j.cad.2020.102867](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2020.102867)). The notes found **no published polynomial G3 N-sided cap**, and no 2015–2026 paper on G3 or variable-radius curvature-continuous rolling-ball fillets. akernel's degree 7 is consistent with a G3 degree budget sitting above the G2 bound of 6. That degree is not proven minimal, and proving a G3 analogue of the 2020 bound is itself a research result.

The concrete akernel move, a proposal built on standard Bernstein bounds, is to **certify seam continuity instead of sampling it**. Across a seam between polynomial patches, the G1 residual (for example det[∂_seam S, ∂_cross S₁, ∂_cross S₂]) is a polynomial in the seam parameter. So are the G2 and G3 residuals once denominators are cleared. Expand each in Bernstein form over the seam interval. The largest absolute coefficient is then a rigorous upper bound (convex-hull property), and evaluating it with akernel's interval or expansion arithmetic turns `geom/continuity.hpp`'s sampled check into a certificate. Converting a numerator bound into an angle or curvature-jump bound also needs a certified *lower* bound on the denominator, such as |n|², which the same machinery supplies. Exact satisfaction is the companion change. Fix the cross-boundary reparametrisation first, and G1–G3 become **linear equality constraints** on control points; the Groisser–Peters matched-Gk result justifies this ([SurfLab list](https://www.cise.ufl.edu/research/SurfLab/pubs.shtml); DOI UNVERIFIED), as does the algebraic G-spline framework ([arXiv 2305.09096](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.09096); authors UNVERIFIED). Solving the energy QP by KKT or a null-space basis then makes constraints hold to rounding, and the certificate bounds the rounding. That is a qualitatively different claim from "0.1°."

For arbitrary N, the notes recommend **Route A**: a central split into N quads, each a degree-7 B-spline like the current square fill, with G3 imposed between sub-patches as constraints and the energy minimised over all N together. The extraordinary centre point is the hard part, and it is where Karčiauskas–Peters' degree bounds apply. Route B (a ribbon patch converted to NURBS per [Salvi, Várady, Rockwood 2021](https://doi.org/10.14733/cadaps.2021.156-169)) is easier to make interactive but is approximate unless the conversion is exact. Its cross-derivative optimisation remains useful for synthesising the higher-order jets that users rarely specify. The same solver covers fillets: a fillet is a 4-sided fill with jets on two rails, corner blends become N-sided fills, and variable radius becomes a rail-placement law. For the energy, **Moreton–Séquin minimum variation** penalises the derivative of normal curvature, which makes it the natural partner of G3 ([10.1145/133994.134035](https://doi.org/10.1145/133994.134035)) [FOUNDATIONAL]. For interactivity, prefactor the sparse KKT once per topology and re-solve only right-hand sides while the user drags, or use Fairing-PIA iteration ([10.1007/s00371-023-02861-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00371-023-02861-7)). Highlight-line refinement (after [Tosun et al. 2007](https://cims.nyu.edu/gcl/papers/tosun2007sou.pdf)) can follow as a nonlinear stage with the exact G-constraints kept hard.

## Batching, not GPUs, is akernel's first performance win

The only substantial GPU SSI line is still Krishnamurthy and McMains (2008–2011). It evaluates dense surface grids, derives per-cell bounds, culls pairs with a GPU AABB hierarchy and refines, and reports SSI **more than 50 times faster than ACIS**, without certification ([thesis](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/59n1g12w); [SPM 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1364901.1364937)) [FOUNDATIONAL]. The notes found no 2020–2026 GPU paper on trimmed-NURBS B-rep Booleans or GPU SSI, and that gap still needs a targeted search. The structure maps onto akernel's interval subdivision provided sampled bounds become rigorous Bernstein enclosures. The speedup comes from data parallelism, so a **structure-of-arrays batch evaluator** (many boxes, one patch) under SIMD should capture most of the CPU-side gain before any GPU port. The BVH pair list and per-pair Krawczyk certification are independent tasks. EMBER's work-stealing design ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3528223.3530181)) and Manifold's TBB parallelism ([GitHub](https://github.com/elalish/manifold)) are the templates, with output sorted by pair ID to keep results deterministic.

The interval-cost advice in the notes needs adjusting for akernel's actual implementation. The literature points to rounding-mode switches (x87 control word or MXCSR read-modify-write) as a dominant constant factor, with AVX-512 embedded rounding as the cure ([Colfax](https://colfaxresearch.com/skl-avx512/); [ARITH-25](https://www.acsel-lab.com/arithmetic/arith25/pdf/18.pdf)). akernel does not switch modes. `num/interval.hpp` computes in round-to-nearest and widens each bound outward with `std::nextafter`. So akernel pays no mode-switch stalls, but it pays a library call per bound and one extra ulp of width per operation, and that width compounds across de Casteljau levels. The measurable options are three. Replace `std::nextafter` with an inline bit-increment on finite values. Evaluate in Bernstein or straight-line-program form, which Breiding, Rose and Timme use to limit overestimation ([arXiv 2011.05000](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.05000v6)). Or move to directed rounding via embedded-rounding intrinsics where available. Each should be measured with a microbenchmark under `bench/`; the notes found no published numbers. Revol and Théveny warn that vendor libraries such as BLAS do not guarantee rounding behaviour inside interval code ([arXiv 1312.3300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3300)) [FOUNDATIONAL]. That rules out an external dense solver inside any certified path, which matters if akernel adopts Busé-style eigen-solving.

On the predicate side, Lévy reports expansion-based predicates "nearly as efficient as plain FP" on classical tests. His Predicate Construction Kit (CAD 2016, seen only in reference lists) generates filtered expansion predicates from formulas ([arXiv 2405.12949](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.12949)), which could replace hand-written ones in `num/predicates.hpp`. The stylistically closest peer is **trueform** (Sajovic and Knez, arXiv 2607.15905, July 2026): header-only C++, exact predicates on the original input planes, and majority voting inside topological units where observations disagree. A snippet of the PDF reports 1000-case medians of **118.0 ms (Manifold), 143.8 ms (EMBER) and 416.9 ms (Cherchi)**, all valid. These are the authors' own measurements and were not confirmed in the full text ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15905)). Its benchmark protocol is worth copying.

For corpora, **ABC** (Koch et al., CVPR 2019, DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2019.00983) offers about 1M Onshape STEP B-reps without history ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333207311_ABC_A_Big_CAD_Model_Dataset_for_Geometric_Deep_Learning)). **Fusion 360 Gallery** (Willis et al., TOG/SIGGRAPH 2021) offers 8,625 human sketch-and-extrude sequences ([arXiv 2010.02392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02392)). Replaying those sequences yields extrude-join, cut and intersect Booleans with a reference result from an industrial kernel, which amounts to **industrial ground truth without a Parasolid licence**. DeepCAD (178,238 sequences, about 115k after deduplication; venue and DOI via aggregators only) adds volume ([STEP-Parts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14927)). Thingi10K matters only for mesh comparisons ([arXiv 1605.04797](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04797)). Better STEP ([arXiv 2506.05417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05417)) and FllumaOne ([arXiv 2606.17696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17696)) are newer B-rep datasets. The notes found **no published robustness comparison between open-source and commercial B-rep kernels**. A rigorous akernel-versus-OCCT table on a fixed ABC subset would therefore be a first, provided it reports validity separately from time and median and tail times rather than means.

## OCCT is the baseline and the STEP front door, not a model

OCCT is the only full open-source NURBS B-rep kernel, and its robustness philosophy is the opposite of akernel's. It **absorbs numerical doubt into growing tolerances**: per-entity tolerances, a user fuzzy value, and gluing modes where the user guarantees coincidence and the documentation cites speedups "up to 90%" ([OCCT spec](https://dev.opencascade.org/doc/overview/html/specification__boolean_operations.html)). Tracker 0026738 (2015) stopped the fuzzy option from inflating input tolerances ([MantisBT](https://tracker.dev.opencascade.org/view.php?id=26738)). Users report that a fuzzy value larger than small features collapses geometry ([opencascade.js #143](https://github.com/donalffons/opencascade.js/discussions/143)). That makes OCCT the right **baseline for failure modes**. The comparison to report is "OCCT valid but tolerance grew to X" against "akernel certified or explicitly refused." Its OBB pre-filter and gluing are cheap ideas to copy, and akernel can detect gluing *exactly* for shared input faces.

The other projects each teach something narrower. CGAL's two-kernel pattern pairs exact predicates with inexact constructions on input and exact constructions where needed, and it is the reference architecture for a predicate layer ([CGAL 6.2](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/PMP_Boolean_operations/index.html)). Manifold's contract, "guaranteed manifold output without caveats or edge cases" or an explicit error, together with its public counterexample policy, matches a certified kernel's posture ([GitHub](https://github.com/elalish/manifold)). Fornjot, a Rust B-rep kernel, was archived on 2026-06-19 with its goals unreached and Booleans never finished ([GitHub](https://github.com/hannobraun/fornjot)). That is a cautionary data point: **topology and validation infrastructure can absorb years before curved Booleans land**. truck (Rust, Apache-2.0) is the nearest open NURBS B-rep peer outside OCCT, with shapeops and stepio crates ([GitHub](https://github.com/ricosjp/truck)). SolveSpace has its own NURBS Booleans ([M-Labs](https://m-labs.hk/software/solvespace)).

For STEP, the fastest credible path is to use OCCT as the reader first. Convert its B-spline and analytic surfaces into akernel types and run the same Booleans in OCCT for comparison. Write a native Part 21 parser later. step-io's approach, which keeps analytic geometry analytic and converts to NURBS only on demand, is the model to follow ([docs.rs step-io](https://docs.rs/step-io)). foxtrot shows which entity subset suffices to *render* real files but samples NURBS rather than handling them exactly ([foxtrot](https://www.mattkeeter.com/projects/foxtrot)). The notes give a minimum entity subset: manifold solid B-rep topology, analytic surfaces and rational B-spline surfaces, `PCURVE`/`SURFACE_CURVE`, and units with `UNCERTAINTY_MEASURE_WITH_UNIT`. That list is **UNVERIFIED against the standard text**. The structural point is firm, though. **Imported edges only meet within the sender's tolerance**, so a certified kernel needs an explicit import boundary. Either it heals by recomputing each edge as a certified SSI of its two adjacent faces, which is akernel's own Krawczyk machinery, or it carries the gap as explicit certified-bounded edge geometry that predicates never consult as an implicit tolerance. Spainhour–Weiss winding numbers classify points robustly on the gappy side of that boundary.

## A six-phase roadmap from six faces to ABC-scale certification

The ordering follows one principle: **do first what is cheap and where akernel is already ahead, and build the evidence harness early so that every later claim is measured.** Phases 1 and 2 turn existing machinery into results no other kernel publishes. Phases 3 to 5 are the long scaling work that any claim of surpassing Parasolid requires. Phase 6 is a parallel surfacing track that can start as soon as Phase 1's seam certificate exists. Realistically, akernel will not surpass Parasolid in breadth. It can surpass it on certified correctness for named configuration classes, and xNURBS on certified continuity order, and both claims can be measured.

Three risks deserve explicit tracking. First, Yang–Jia–Yan's method may rest on floating-point thresholds, and in that case akernel must certify its special-point step independently rather than copy it. Second, SSI-specific separation bounds are unknown, so the frequency of "perturbed-system only" answers in Phase 2 is an empirical question. Third, Fornjot's fate shows that Phase 3's topology work can swallow a project, which argues for keeping the 6-face suite and the oracles green at every step.

## Black-box comparison with xNURBS can itself be certified

The key observation is that **an exported NURBS surface is exact data**. Its control points, weights and knots are doubles, which are dyadic rationals. akernel can therefore run the same Bernstein-form seam certificates on xNURBS's exported surfaces that it runs on its own. The comparison does not have to rely on sampling, and it never needs to inspect xNURBS binaries. The protocol needs care at only three points. Both tools must receive bit-identical boundary data, so export akernel's surrounding faces as NURBS to Rhino and check the round trip by re-exporting a known surface and comparing control points exactly. xNURBS output must be exported with full precision (17 significant digits in text formats). And the xNURBS licence should be checked for benchmarking terms before results are published; this report could not verify those terms.

The test cases should separate capability from quality. A **reproduction set** cuts a hole in a single known surface (a polynomial Bezier patch, a sphere, a torus) where the ideal fill is the original surface; deviation from it is a clean, energy-independent score. A **symmetric N-gon set** (N = 3, 4, 5, 6) with G2 and with G3-capable surroundings isolates the extraordinary-point problem. An **asymmetric and degenerate set** adds skewed corners, a very short edge, near-180° corners, high aspect ratio and boundaries whose cross-curvature changes sign. A **domain set** adds concave and holed loops, which a square-domain patch cannot represent. A **constraint set** adds interior points and curves, which xNURBS advertises. A **blend set** covers a three-fillet corner and a variable-radius fillet treated as a 4-sided fill. Each case runs at xNURBS's documented level (G2), and akernel also runs at G3 so that the extra order is shown rather than claimed.

The measurements fall into five groups. **Seam continuity jumps**, certified on both tools' outputs with Bernstein bounds and also reported as distributions along each seam, cover the positional gap, the maximum normal angle (G1, with 0.1° as the industry reference), the relative jump in normal curvature in the cross-boundary direction and in the full shape operator (G2), and the jump in the arc-length derivative of normal curvature across the seam (G3). **Curvature behaviour** covers principal-curvature ranges, sign changes of Gaussian curvature, umbilic counts, comb monotonicity across each seam (Alias's "no peak at the boundary" criterion, made numeric), and highlight-line oscillation counts under a fixed light set, which is Karčiauskas–Peters' class-A criterion made countable. **Fairness energies** must be computed geometrically so that they do not depend on parameterisation: ∫(κ₁²+κ₂²)dA as a thin-plate measure and Moreton–Séquin's ∫((∂κ₁/∂e₁)²+(∂κ₂/∂e₂)²)dA as the minimum-variation measure, both by high-order quadrature on the exported surface. Parametric energies cannot be compared across tools whose parameterisations differ, and xNURBS's own energy is not public. **Patch structure** covers patch count per fill, degree, knot vectors and interior knot multiplicities (which bound internal continuity), control-point count, weight range for rational output, trimmed versus untrimmed output, and parameterisation quality (Jacobian ratio). **Constraint fidelity and responsiveness** cover deviation at interior constraints, the fraction of cases producing a valid surface, and wall time through each tool's documented interface, with xNURBS's millisecond claim tested via its V5 API if the licence allows.

This protocol has an asymmetry the reader should keep in mind. It can show that akernel's fills meet higher continuity orders with certified bounds and lower geometric fairness energies on identical inputs. It cannot reveal *why* xNURBS behaves as it does, and it should not be presented as if it could.

## Conclusion

The survey's most useful finding is where the gaps sit. Neither the academic literature nor any commercial kernel offers certification at the two places where akernel's architecture already points: tangent and degenerate SSI, and curvature-continuous seams. In both, the missing pieces are established mathematics applied in a new place, not new theory. Deflation feeds an existing Krawczyk operator, and Bernstein bounds feed an existing continuity check. That makes these the highest-leverage, lowest-risk moves. They also change the competitive framing. Parasolid, ACIS, OCCT and xNURBS all report quality as a measured deviation inside a tolerance. akernel can report a proof, or an explicit refusal with a perturbation bound, and that is a different category of answer, not a better number on the same scale.

The other implication is that "surpassing" has to be defined before it can be achieved. The literature offers no accepted B-rep robustness benchmark against commercial kernels. Fusion 360 Gallery replays and an ABC subset, scored on validity separately from speed with OCCT's tolerance growth reported alongside, would let akernel define the benchmark rather than chase one. The same logic applies to xNURBS. Because exported NURBS are exact data, akernel can be the first tool to publish *certified* continuity and fairness numbers for a competitor's surfaces. That turns a closed-source marketing claim into a reproducible measurement.
